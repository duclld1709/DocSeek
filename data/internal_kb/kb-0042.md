# Service ownership and on-call { #service-ownership-and-on-call }

## Overview { #overview }

At Acme Payments, maintaining service stability requires clear accountability. Every service must list its owning team in the service catalog. This registry serves as the source of truth for routing incident alerts, reviewing pull requests, and managing infrastructure deployments. If a service lacks a designated owner, it is considered unmaintained and must be assigned to the appropriate engineering team immediately.

## On-call rotations { #on-call-rotations }

Each team is responsible for managing its own on-call rotation to support the production environment. To ensure consistency across the organization, all on-call rotations are exactly one week long. The handover process occurs every Monday at 10:00 local time. 

During the handover, the outgoing engineer must brief the incoming engineer on any unresolved incidents, active alerts, or pending operational tasks. The incoming engineer assumes full responsibility for the rotation upon the completion of this handover. PagerDuty schedules must be updated to reflect these transitions at least 24 hours prior to the handover time to avoid gaps in coverage.

## Incident response { #incident-response }

The sre-team provides platform-level support, but primary operational ownership remains with the team listed in the service catalog. When an alert triggers, the on-call engineer should utilize the service-specific runbooks found in our internal documentation. If an incident requires cross-team coordination, the on-call engineer is responsible for initiating the communication bridge and escalating to the sre-team if the incident impacts core payment processing infrastructure. Regular review of on-call performance is conducted during team retrospectives to identify recurring technical debt.
