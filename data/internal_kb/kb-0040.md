# Postmortem of the July 2026 expired TLS certificate { #postmortem-of-the-july-2026-expired-tls-certificate }

## Summary { #summary }

On July 14, 2026, the public API experienced a total outage lasting 18 minutes. The incident was triggered by the expiration of the primary TLS certificate terminating traffic on our edge load balancers. This resulted in client-side connection errors for all incoming requests. The sre-team successfully restored service by manually deploying a replacement certificate.

## Timeline { #timeline }

The outage began at 09:12 UTC when monitoring alerts indicated a spike in 5xx error rates. Engineering teams identified the expired TLS certificate at 09:22 UTC. A new certificate was provisioned and propagated across the edge cluster, with service restoration confirmed at 09:30 UTC. The total duration of the service disruption was 18 minutes.

## Root cause { #root-cause }

The incident occurred because the legacy certificate management process relied on a manual update schedule. The specific certificate failed to undergo rotation before its expiration date, causing the load balancer to reject all TLS handshakes. Existing monitoring failed to provide sufficient lead time to address the near-term expiration, leading to the reactive manual intervention required during the outage.

## Action items { #action-items }

To prevent recurrence, the sre-team has implemented a fully automated certificate management lifecycle. Certificates are now renewed automatically 30 days before expiry, removing the dependency on manual tracking. Furthermore, we have updated our alerting thresholds to trigger critical notifications 45 days prior to expiration. These changes ensure that certificate rotation is handled programmatically without human intervention, maintaining the availability of the public API.
